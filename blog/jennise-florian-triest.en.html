Walking Weddings Journal Issue №13 · MMXXVI

Adriatic Love Story

Jennise & Florian — A Summer Wedding in Trieste

Jennise & Florian Trieste, Italy Photo + Film

Trieste Adriatic Love Summer Rain First Look Trieste Adriatic Love Summer Rain First Look

Chapter I
Prologue

There are places where love is not merely celebrated but lived — cities that seem built from longing itself. Trieste is such a place. An Italian coastal town with an Austrian soul, set between sea and history, where the Adriatic nestles against ancient quay walls and every sunset feels like a promise. Here, in this city that once inspired James Joyce and moved Rainer Maria Rilke, Jennise and Florian said their vows — an Austrian couple who crossed borders for their wedding and, in the Italian summer heat, created a day that shimmered between tears, rain, and golden light.

Their story began in the morning at the Hotel Ristorante Tre Merli, a house that would later also become the setting for the celebration. As the bride slipped into her dress, a delicate tension already hung in the air — that blend of anticipation and nerves that makes every wedding morning something magical.

Chapter II
The First Look by the Sea

The first moment in which bride and groom see each other before the ceremony is always a promise — a quiet, private ritual before the world joins in. For this, Jennise and Florian chose a jetty reaching out into the sea, surrounded by the salty breeze of the Adriatic. It was a moment in which time seemed to stand still. Florian turned around, and what followed was not a staged scene but a genuine pause — tears, smiles, a long embrace. The love between them needs no words.

Yet there was a second first look, just as moving: Jennise stepped before her father and her brother. The men who had watched her grow up, who had held and protected her, now saw her as a bride — and the emotions overwhelmed them. Tears flowed, embraces lingered, and in that moment it became clear that a wedding is not only the union of two people but the coming together of families, stories, memories.

Chapter III
The Ceremony in San Bartolomeo

The church of San Bartolomeo in Barcola is a place that breathes stories — a sacred space in which generations before Jennise and Florian made their vows. As the bride walked down the aisle, arm in arm with her father, Florian burst into tears. It was no discreet swallowing, no suppressed trembling, but an open, unguarded weeping — the kind of emotion that cannot be feigned. In that moment he was no longer the groom waiting for his bride, but a man watching the happiness of his life come toward him.

The ceremony was classic, solemn, moving. Words were spoken that are older than the church itself, and as the rings were exchanged, time beyond these walls seemed to matter no longer. After the vows came the send-off — a ritual in which the guests threw rice and released the newlyweds into their new life with cheers and confetti. The sun stood high, the mood was exuberant, and for a moment everything seemed perfect.

Chapter IV
Rain, Umbrellas, and Romance

There is an Italian saying: Sposa bagnata, sposa fortunata — a wet bride is a lucky bride. And so, as if the weather itself wished to prove this wisdom, it began to rain. Not softly, not quietly, but with a determination that threw every plan into disarray. The wedding party withdrew to the Ristorante Tre Merli, and while the guests gathered beneath awnings, Jennise and Florian brought out their transparent umbrellas — and decided not to cancel the couple shoot but to embrace it.

The images created in those minutes are among the most beautiful of the day. Rain on umbrellas, wet streets reflecting the light, and a couple laughing as if the heavens were on their side. The bridesmaids joined in with umbrellas of their own, and suddenly the rain was no longer an obstacle but a gift — a reminder that perfection lies not in control but in letting go.

The rain was not an obstacle but a gift — a reminder that beauty lies in the unexpected.

— Walking Weddings

Chapter V
Sunset and Dolce Vita

And then, as if by a miracle, the rain stopped. The clouds parted, and the sun returned — not shyly, but triumphantly, in that golden light only an Italian summer evening can offer. It was the perfect backdrop for the cake cutting. Jennise and Florian cut the cake together, surrounded by their loved ones, and as they kissed, the world seemed to stand still for a moment.

But that was not all. For what would an Italian celebration be without gelato? Instead of classic desserts there was authentic Italian ice cream, served in little bowls, and the guests savoured it in the warm evening light. It was a moment of pure Dolce Vita, a feeling that cannot be staged — the joy of the simple, of pleasure, of togetherness. Jennise and Florian had not merely celebrated a wedding but created a memory that runs deeper than any timeline, any plan, any expectation.
