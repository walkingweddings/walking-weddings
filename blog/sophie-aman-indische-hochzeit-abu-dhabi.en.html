Walking Weddings Journal Issue №01 · MMXXVI

Desert Ritual

An Indian Wedding in Abu Dhabi

Sophie & Aman Abu Dhabi, UAE Photo + Film

Abu Dhabi Indian Traditions Mandap Fairmont Abu Dhabi Indian Traditions Mandap Fairmont

Chapter I
Prologue

There are days that cannot be measured in hours — days carried by rituals older than memory itself. Sophie and Aman's wedding in Abu Dhabi was one such day. At the Fairmont Hotel, beneath a sky that revealed all its nuances between dawn and dusk, traditions, colours and emotions merged into a single, sacred moment. From the Choora Ceremony, in which Sophie's wrists were adorned with brilliant red bangles, through the Sehra Bandi that prepared Aman for the ceremony, to the magnificent mandap that rose like a Garden of Eden in the desert — every instant was filled with meaning, with devotion, with a love that spanned cultures and continents.

Chapter II
The Choora Ceremony

The day began in the stillness of a hotel room, the air heavy with expectation. Sophie sat surrounded by the women of her family as the traditional red choora bangles — symbols of fortune, fertility and new beginnings — were placed upon her wrists. Each bangle was bestowed with a blessing, with a whisper that had outlasted generations. Sophie's eyes shone, not only with the beauty of the moment, but with the depth of its meaning. It was as though, in that instant, she became not merely a bride, but part of an ancient story — a story of women who had walked this path before her.

The choora is more than adornment. It is a promise, a bridge between past and future. And as the bangles gleamed on Sophie's wrists, we knew: this day would surpass every expectation.

Chapter III
Sehra Bandi & Ribbon Ceremony

While Sophie was being wrapped in the choora, Aman prepared for his own ceremony. During the Sehra Bandi, the traditional floral veil was placed upon him — a gesture of protection, of humility and of transition. Surrounded by his closest friends and family, who accompanied him with songs and blessings, Aman was transformed into the groom ready to receive his bride. The sehra, which half-concealed his face, lent him an almost mythical aura — as though he were speaking to us from another age.

In parallel, the Ribbon Ceremony took place, a symbolic act that bound the two families together. A long red ribbon was passed from hand to hand among the relatives, a silent promise that this wedding united not only two people, but two worlds. It was a moment full of stillness and movement at once — a dance of connection that held us all in its spell.

It was as though the desert itself stood still to make room for this moment.

— Walking Weddings

Chapter IV
The Mandap

The mandap, the sacred pavilion where the ceremony was to take place, was a work of art in itself. Built upon the elegant steps of the Fairmont Hotel, it rose like a portal between earth and sky. Tropical flowers in vivid shades of pink, orange and red wound around the columns, while white blossoms struck a contrast that at once soothed and captivated the eye. Every flower was hand-chosen, every arrangement a prayer. The mandap was not merely a backdrop, but the heartbeat of the ceremony — a place where heaven and earth touched.

As Sophie descended the steps, the world held its breath. Her wedding gown shimmered, embroidered with golden threads that caught the light and cast it back. Her gaze was fixed on Aman, who waited for her at the foot of the staircase. In that moment nothing else existed — no guests, no camera, no time. Only her, him, and the promise they were about to give one another.

Chapter V
The Ceremony

At the heart of the mandap burned the sacred fire, Agni, the divine witness to all vows. Sophie and Aman circled it seven times, each round a step along their shared path, each round a commitment: love, respect, fidelity, trust. The priests chanted in Sanskrit, their voices merging with the crackle of the flames and the soft whisper of the wind. The guests, dressed in colourful saris and sherwanis, followed the ceremony with a reverence that hushed even the loudest moment into silence.

When the vows had been spoken, when the petals had been scattered over the couple, when the families had given their blessings, it was as though the desert itself stood still to make room for this moment. Sophie and Aman were no longer two individuals — they were one, bound by rituals older than any border we humans had ever drawn.

Chapter VI
The Celebration

After the ceremony, the gates to the celebration opened — and with them a wave of colour, music and joie de vivre broke over us. Guests from all over the world, from the most varied cultures and traditions, came together to celebrate Sophie and Aman. Traditional dances alternated with modern rhythms; moving speeches brought both laughter and tears. The food, a feast for all the senses, married Indian spices with Arabic influences — a culinary ode to the merging of two worlds.

Far into the night, people danced, laughed and celebrated. The Fairmont Hotel, usually a place of calm and elegance, was transformed into a palace of joy. And as we lowered our cameras, as the last lights went out, we knew: this day would live on not only in our images. It would live on in the hearts of all those who had the privilege of being there.
